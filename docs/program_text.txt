Program
Bachelor's Track Program: Earth and
Environmental Sciences for Sustainability
1. Open Doors winner's skill set
The winner should know:
the structure and characteristics of the Earth's geospheres and biosphere;
the geological processes occurring on the surface and in the subsurface;
general information about the global interconnections between the components of the biosphere and
the circulation of substances;
the principles of the interaction of living organisms with the environment;
the structure of ecosystems and the biosphere as a global ecosystem;
the basic chemical reactions of the carbon cycle and the biogeochemical balance;
global environmental issues and the goals of sustainable development for humanity, as well as
modern sustainability strategies: measures to prevent and adapt to climate change, resource
conservation, the "carbon economy", and the "circular economy". 
The winner should be able to:
assess the availability of resources for human needs; 
identify minerals and rocks; 
use geological maps, sections, and descriptions to determine the sequence of rock formation; 
assess the condition of natural objects by comparing their characteristics with speciﬁed standards; 
establish cause-and-eﬀect relationships between human impacts on natural systems and changes in
their condition.

3. Content
Field of science 1. Geography, physical
Geography
1. Planet Earth, its structure, composition and history of development: basic concepts of the formation,
structure and composition of the Earth.
2. Fundamentals of cartography, geoinformatics, and geoecology. Thematic maps (geographical,
topographic, tectonic, structural, etc.).
3. Landscapes and relief: the concept of relief, endogenous processes, exogenous processes, relief-
forming factors, types of relief.
4. Obtaining information about the Earth's surface using satellites, drones, or aerial photography.
Field of science 2. Meteorology & atmospheric sciences
Geography
1. Structure, properties and functions of the atmosphere: concepts of the atmosphere structure, the
component composition of the modern atmosphere and the history of its formation, the role of water
vapor in creating a microclimate. The ozone layer.
2. Methods for studying the atmosphere state. Sustainable Development goals related to atmospheric
quality.
3. Climatology and meteorology.
4. Modern climate and problems of its change: concepts of weather and climate; understanding of
meteorological conditions and their role in the functioning of living systems and economic objects.
Physics
1. Changes in atmospheric temperature with altitude; processes of movement of air masses in the
atmosphere.
Field of science 3. Geology
Geography
1. Planet Earth, its structure, composition and history of development: basic concepts of the formation,
structure and composition of the Earth.
2. Tectonics and structural geology, basic concepts of the tectonic structure and evolution of the Earth.
3. Minerals: basic information about the varieties and classiﬁcations of minerals; basic concepts of the
formation, structure and composition of the Earth.
4. The most common minerals and rocks; visual characteristics of the main minerals and rocks; resource
availability and prevalence of the main types of minerals by country and continent.
5. Exploration and geological and economic assessment of mineral deposits: a type of minerals.

Mathematics
1. Flat and three-dimensional geometric shapes and their spatial relationship, their main properties.
Field of science 4. Geochemistry & geophysics
Physics
1. Geophysics: the relationship between geology and physics; basic understanding of physical processes
and physical properties on Earth.
2. The eﬀect in the universe of physical laws discovered in terrestrial conditions.
3. Physical phenomena, processes, patterns and laws; theoretical and applied issues of geophysics;
geophysical methods of studying the Earth and prospecting for minerals.
Chemistry
1. Chemical elements and their compounds; chemical and physical properties of natural substances
(minerals, rocks), their distribution in the Earth and on its surface.
2. Geochemistry: the relationship between geology and chemistry, patterns and laws, basic academic
and applied problems of geochemistry, chemical reactions of the carbon cycle.
Field of science 5. Mining & mineral processing
Geography
1. Minerals: basic information about the varieties and classiﬁcations of minerals; basic concepts of the
formation, structure and composition of the Earth.
2. Exploration and geological and economic assessment of mineral deposits: a type of minerals.
3. Mining and the impact on the environment: basic chemical and physical concepts, patterns, laws and
theories; methods of extraction of solid, liquid and gaseous minerals.
4. Geography of extraction of key types of minerals by continents and countries; negative mining and
geological processes; impact of mining operations on the environment, the main types of waste
generated during mining.
Mathematics
1. Statistical patterns in the real world.
2. Properties of geometric shapes and formulas for solving problems related to calculating volumes,
percentages of useful components, and economic indicators.
Chemistry
1. Mineral processing: basic chemical and physical concepts, patterns, laws and theories; general
information about mineral processing.
