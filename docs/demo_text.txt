Solution method:
Oxidizing agent and reducing agent: 
Oxidizing agent: O₂ (oxygen). The oxidation state of oxygen decreases from 0 to –2 (in H₂O and
sulfates) — reduction process. 
Reducing agent: FeS₂ (pyrite), more precisely — sulfur in its composition. The oxidation state of
sulfur increases from –1 (in S₂²⁻) to +6 (in SO₄²⁻) — oxidation process. Iron retains the oxidation state

Amount of substance of pyrite: n(FeS₂) = m / M = 24 000 g / 120 g/mol = 200 mol 
According to the reaction equation: 2 mol FeS₂ produce 2 mol H₂SO₄, meaning the ratio is 1:1.
Therefore, n(H₂SO₄) = 200 mol 
Mass of sulfuric acid: m(H₂SO₄) = n × M = 200 mol × 98 g/mol = 19 600 g = 19.6 kg

According to the equation: 1 mol H₂SO₄ reacts with 1 mol Ca(OH)₂, meaning: n(Ca(OH)₂) = n(H₂SO₄) =
200 mol 
Mass of pure slaked lime: m(Ca(OH)₂) = n × M = 200 mol × 74 g/mol = 14 800 g = 14.8 kg

Oral-response task (maximum 5 points)
Field of science: Geography, physical
Advanced-level task: 41
Name the geographical factors that inﬂuence sunshine duration. What does sunshine duration
depend on?
Answer:  Sunshine duration is the time (in hours) during which the sun is above the horizon and
unobscured (for example, by dense clouds). The main geographic factors that inﬂuence it include:
Latitude: Determines the sun's height above the horizon and the length of daylight. At polar latitudes, polar
day (the sun shines 24 hours a day) occurs in summer, and polar night occurs in winter.
Cloud cover: The main factor reducing the amount of direct sunlight. Subtropical deserts (e.g., the Sahara)
experience the least cloud cover and, consequently, the longest sunshine duration.
Topography: Mountain ranges can shade an area, reducing sunshine hours in valleys. In addition, the
aspect of the slopes is important (slopes facing the equator receive more sunlight). 
Proximity to bodies of water and oceans: In coastal areas, sea fog and increased cloud cover often form,
which reduces the number of hours of sunshine compared to inland areas.

The air becomes more transparent, which increases the duration and intensity of sunshine. 
Atmospheric circulation and haze: The presence of suspended particles (dust, smoke, aerosols, forest

Oral-response task (maximum 5 points)
Field of science: Geochemistry & geophysics
Advanced-level task: 42
What is an oxidation reaction? 
Answer:  An oxidation reaction is a chemical process in which an atom, ion, or molecule loses electrons,
leading to an increase in its oxidation state. A classic example of oxidation is the interaction of a substance
with oxygen, for example, combustion or rusting of iron. However, in the modern understanding, oxidation
can also proceed without the participation of oxygen, with the involvement of other substances, such as
halogens or metal ions. Oxidation reactions are always coupled with reduction reactions: together they form
redox processes in which electrons transfer from the reducing agent to the oxidizing agent.
